    Networks and the PID policy¶

    An agent's networks are set by its constructor: the hidden layers as a tuple of strings, initialisers by name, optional normalisation and an image encoder. The actor can also be a PID controller whose gains the agent learns.

    Hidden layers¶

    An architecture is a tuple read left to right. A number adds a dense layer of that width; a name adds an activation:

    from ajax import PPO

agent = PPO(
    "CartPole-v1",
    n_envs=8,
    n_steps=128,
    actor_architecture=("64", "tanh", "64", "tanh"),
    critic_architecture=("256", "relu", "256", "relu"),
    encoder_kernel_init="orthogonal(np.sqrt(2))",  # the hidden layers' kernels
    critic_bias_init="0.0",  # a number gives a constant initialiser
    encoder_layer_norm=True,  # a LayerNorm after the hidden layers
)
state, _ = agent.train(seed=0, n_timesteps=2_048)

    The activations are relu, tanh, leaky_relu, swish, silu and mish; a callable works too. "layernorm" adds a LayerNorm where it stands, so ("256", "layernorm", "relu", "256", "layernorm", "relu") is RLPD's critic (Ball et al., 2023). The agent adds its output heads after the hidden layers: the policy's mean and standard deviation, the value, the Q-values. DQN and PQN take a single architecture for their Q-network.

    Each agent's default follows its paper or reference implementation:

    Default Agents
    ("256", "relu", "256", "relu") SAC, ASAC, REDQ, TD3
    ("256", "leaky_relu", "256", "leaky_relu") AVG
    ("128", "tanh", "128", "tanh") PPO
    ("64", "tanh", "64", "tanh") APO
    ("128", "relu", "128", "relu") DQN, PQN, UDRL, APG (its controller)

    DreamerV3 and TD-MPC2 build their own networks to their papers' specifications: see World models.

    Initialisers¶

    Kernels start from an orthogonal initialiser with gain 1 and biases from zero, except the policy's output layer (the mean, or the logits for discrete actions), whose kernel starts from orthogonal(0.01): a near-zero first mean, or a near-uniform first policy (TD3's deterministic actor starts its last layer uniform in ±3e-3, as DDPG does), and APO's hidden layers, which start as its reference code's ("torch_linear", below). Initialisers are named by string:

    • "orthogonal(0.01)", "lecun_uniform", or any other factory in flax.linen.initializers, with its argument in brackets (np, jnp and math may appear in it, as in "orthogonal(np.sqrt(2))");
    • "zeros", and "trunc_normal_fan_in(1.0)" (DreamerV3's initialiser);
    • a number such as "0.0" or "-1.0", for a constant.

    PPO and APO take three of them: encoder_kernel_init for the hidden layers' kernels, actor_bias_init for the policy head's bias and critic_bias_init for the value head's. PPO also takes mean_kernel_init (below). encoder_kernel_init="torch_linear" starts each hidden layer as torch's nn.Linear does: its kernel and its bias both uniform in ±1/sqrt(fan_in), the fan-in being the layer's input width.

    Layer normalisation¶

    The hidden layers form an encoder that the output heads read. encoder_layer_norm=True adds a LayerNorm on its output. It is off by default, because the SAC, TD3, PPO and DQN papers use plain MLPs. PPO takes it as an argument. SAC, ASAC, REDQ, APO and DQN, and TD3's critics (not its actor), read it from the network configuration, which you can set before training:

    from ajax import SAC

agent = SAC("Pendulum-v1", learning_starts=500)
agent.network_args = agent.network_args.replace(encoder_layer_norm=True)
state, _ = agent.train(seed=0, n_timesteps=1_000)

    UDRL turns it on for its policy and APG's controller always keeps a LayerNorm on its encoder, as Ajax's earlier networks did. PQN puts one after every hidden layer, as its paper does, whatever the setting. AVG normalises its penultimate features instead, as its paper does, which overrides the setting.

    The policy head¶

    PPO exposes the policy head's options. By default its Gaussian learns one log standard deviation per action dimension, whatever the observation, starting at 0 (a standard deviation of 1) and never clipped (log_std_bounds=None), as PPO's reference code does: OpenAI Baselines at ea25b9e (ppo2's common/distributions.py:96-106; ppo1's ppo1/mlp_policy.py:36-39, with gaussian_fixed_var=True), CleanRL at fe8d8a0 (ppo_continuous_action.py:129) and PureJaxRL at 31756b1 (ppo_continuous_action.py:41). log_std_state_independent=False with log_std_init=-1.0 builds the head PPO had before, SAC's: a log standard deviation computed from the observation, which log_std_bounds=(-20.0, 2.0) clips as SAC's is. APO sets its reference code's head (xtma/apo, built on the authors' rlpyt fork): one learned log standard deviation per dimension, starting at 0 and unclipped by default (log_std_bounds=None), around a mean passed through tanh (tanh_mean), with an unsquashed sample; its mean layer is lecun_uniform. The other agents clip theirs, a PID policy's included, to (-20.0, 2.0).

    Argument Default (PPO) Effect
    log_std_state_independent True one learned log standard deviation per action dimension; False computes it from the observation
    log_std_init 0.0 the initial log standard deviation
    log_std_bounds None the (low, high) clip of the log standard deviation, whichever head (or PID policy) learns it, low < high (the agent, its network config and the actor refuse others); None leaves it unclipped
    mean_kernel_init None: orthogonal(0.01), a near-zero first mean the initialiser of the mean's output layer
    squash False tanh on the sampled action, with the matching log-probability correction (Squashing)

    Squashing¶

    SAC, TD3, REDQ, ASAC, AVG and UDRL's continuous policy, and PPO's with squash=True, act through tanh, which keeps actions in [-1, 1]. The squashing is one wrapper, not an option of each head: Squashed(actor) wraps any actor, and its policy is squash(pi) of the actor's: Squash(pi) for a distribution, tanh of the action for TD3's deterministic one. Squashed shares the actor's parameters, so they are saved and read under the same names as the actor's alone.

    Squash (in ajax.networks.distributions) takes any distrax distribution, its latent, pi.distribution. Sampling and log-probabilities are exact. log_prob(action) inverts tanh, which float32 cannot do once the action rounds to ±1; log_prob_from_raw(raw) reads the pre-tanh sample instead, as a squashed PPO does when it recomputes a log-probability. Two quantities have no closed form: mean() is tanh of the latent's mean, the action the agent evaluates with, not the mean of the squashed action; entropy(seed) is a one-sample estimate, the latent's entropy plus the tanh log-det-Jacobian at a sample, as brax estimates it. The functions latent, sample_with_raw, log_prob_from_raw and entropy read any policy, squashed or not, so code that uses them works with both.

    Image observations¶

    PPO, DQN, PQN and UDRL can read images through a convolutional encoder. Ajax flattens image observations, so give the image's shape (height, width, channels) as cnn_image_shape; the encoder reshapes the flat vector back. cnn_extra_obs_dim counts extra scalar entries after the image, which join the image features after the convolutions. cnn_spec sets the convolutions:

    from ajax import PQN
from ajax.networks.networks import CNNSpec

# MinAtar observations are 10 x 10 images with 4 channels.
agent = PQN(
    "Breakout-MinAtar",
    cnn_image_shape=(10, 10, 4),
    cnn_spec=CNNSpec(channels=(16, 32), kernel_sizes=(3, 3), strides=(1, 1), feature_dim=128),
)
state, _ = agent.train(seed=0, n_timesteps=2_048)

    DQN and PQN default to their reference codes' networks (see their pages); PPO and UDRL to CNNSpec(). CNNSpec() with no arguments is this encoder: two ReLU convolutions of 16 and 32 channels (kernels 4 and 3, stride 2, "SAME" padding), then a 128-wide dense layer, all at flax's default initialisation. The image is read in height, width, channels order. padding takes flax's padding ("VALID" for none), layer_norm=True puts a LayerNorm after every layer, before its ReLU, and kernel_init names the initialiser of every layer, as the network's other *_kernel_init settings do.

    The PID policy¶

    Many control tasks are run by PID controllers, which engineers can read, tune and certify. The PID policy makes the actor such a controller: its action is

    u = Kp e + Ki I + Kd D

    where e, I and D are the tracking error, its integral and its difference, which PIDTermsWrapper puts first in the observation. The agent learns the gains. Pass a PIDPolicy as the actor_architecture of any continuous-action agent: SAC, TD3, REDQ, ASAC, AVG, PPO or APO.

    import jax
import jax.numpy as jnp
from flax import struct
from gymnax.environments import environment, spaces

from ajax import SAC
from ajax.environments.pid_terms import PIDTermsWrapper
from ajax.modules.pid_actor import PIDPolicy, pid_gains


@struct.dataclass
class State(environment.EnvState):
    x: jax.Array  # the controlled value
    r: jax.Array  # its set-point


@struct.dataclass
class Params(environment.EnvParams):
    max_steps_in_episode: int = 10


class Setpoint(environment.Environment):
    """An integrator, x <- x + 4u, that must reach a random set-point r."""

    @property
    def default_params(self):
        return Params()

    def reset_env(self, key, params):
        r = jax.random.uniform(key, minval=-1.0, maxval=1.0)
        state = State(time=0, x=jnp.float32(0.0), r=r)
        return self.get_obs(state), state

    def step_env(self, key, state, action, params):
        x = state.x + 4.0 * jnp.clip(action[0], -1.0, 1.0)
        state = state.replace(x=x, time=state.time + 1)
        return self.get_obs(state), state, -((state.r - x) ** 2), jnp.array(False), {}

    def get_obs(self, state, params=None, key=None):
        return jnp.stack([state.x, state.r])

    def observation_space(self, params):
        return spaces.Box(-jnp.inf, jnp.inf, (2,))

    def action_space(self, params):
        return spaces.Box(-1.0, 1.0, (1,))


# The observation becomes [e, I, D, x, r], with e = r - x.
env = PIDTermsWrapper(Setpoint(), current_idx=(0,), target_idx=(1,), dt=1.0)
agent = SAC(
    env,
    env_params=Params(),
    n_envs=4,
    actor_architecture=PIDPolicy(n_errors=1, gains="fixed"),
    critic_architecture=("64", "relu", "64", "relu"),
    actor_learning_rate=3e-3,
    critic_learning_rate=1e-3,
    gamma=0.9,
    learning_starts=500,
)
state, _ = agent.train(seed=[0, 1], n_timesteps=4_000, num_episode_test=4)
gains = pid_gains(state.actor_state.params)  # {"kp", "ki", "kd"}, one value per seed
print({name: value.squeeze() for name, value in gains.items()})

    The best controller here is Kp = 1/4, Ki = Kd = 0, which reaches the set-point in one step; the repository's tests check that SAC and PPO find it with longer training.

    PIDPolicy has two kinds of gains:

    • gains="fixed" makes the gains the actor's only parameters: a linear map with no bias and no hidden layer. The trained policy deploys as a plain PID with the gains pid_gains reads off it, in the agent's units (below) (Lawrence et al., 2022, tune PID gains with reinforcement learning the same way).
    • gains="observation" predicts the gains from the observation with an MLP of architecture, as gain scheduling does. Read them for given observations with PIDActor(policy, action_dim).apply(params, obs, method="gains"), an array of shape (..., 3, action_dim, n_errors); the parameters of a squashed PID actor are the same.

    n_errors is the number of errors the wrapper tracks; with several, each action dimension gets its own gain on each error. The gains start at zero: the untrained controller commands the centre of the action box, which is no action on a symmetric box. The stochastic agents explore with a learned standard deviation that does not depend on the observation, clipped as the agent's other heads are (log_std_bounds: none in PPO and APO by default, [-20, 2] in the others).

    Each agent's own output rule applies to u. SAC, TD3, REDQ, ASAC and AVG squash it with tanh and APO takes tanh(u) as its mean, so the deployed controller is a saturated PID; APO samples unsquashed around tanh(u), PPO around u (unless squash=True), and their samples are clipped to [-1, 1]. Like every action, u is in the agent's units and is then mapped onto the environment's bounds (Actions): the deployed controller is c + h · sat(u), with c the centre of the action box, h its half-width and sat the agent's tanh or clip. The learned gains are therefore normalised: a PID written in the environment's units has gains h · K (for small signals, where tanh is close to linear) and the offset c. Observation normalisation leaves the error terms raw, so the gains act on errors in the environment's units, while the gain network of gains="observation" reads the rest of the observation normalised.

    A PID policy needs a continuous action space and takes no memory: its memory is the integral and the difference in the observation.

    Two other PID features are separate from this one. APG's in-context controller ends with a learnable PID layer on its network's output: see APG. SAC's use_pid_policy lets SAC tune the gains of an expert controller: see Expert guidance.
