    Expert guidance on Pendulum¶

    Take a controller that swings Pendulum up well but not perfectly. Does each of four published ways of using it make SAC learn faster than SAC alone?

    The question, the controller, the arms and the test were registered before any arm ran, in benchmarks/expert_guidance/PREREG.md. Each seed's score is the mean of its 20 evaluation returns over 20,000 steps (ajax.compare.auc). AdaStop compared each arm with SAC alone, with a family-wise error of 0.05, 4 seeds per arm and interim, at most 12 interims.

    Verdicts¶

    Arm Against SAC alone Seeds Difference in score [95%]
    A1 ExpertPrefill (DDPGfD) no difference found 48 +2.6 [-4.3, +9.4]
    A2 JSRLCurriculum (JSRL) lower 8 -308.5 [-369.7, -236.4]
    A3 IBRL higher 16 +21.8 [+12.1, +31.1]
    A4 ResidualPolicy (residual RL) higher 8 +88.3 [+78.2, +97.3]
    • A1 ran to the last interim without AdaStop telling it apart from SAC alone. That does not show the two equal: a gap of 10.7 or more would have been found 80% of the time (the resolution), a smaller one could be missed.
    • A2 scored lower, decided after two interims.
    • A3 scored higher, decided after four.
    • A4 scored higher, decided after two.

    What each score measures¶

    Each evaluation plays the arm's deterministic policy as the method deploys it:

    • A0, A1 and A2 act with the policy alone.
    • A3 acts as IBRL does, with the better of the policy's action and the expert's, by the target critics.
    • A4 executes the expert's action plus the policy's, clipped to the action range.

    A2's expert acts during collection only. Its curriculum hands whole episodes to the policy at step 10,000, half the run, which is also A2's median step to -200. A1's 2,000 expert steps fill the replay before training and come on top of the 20,000.

    How it ran¶

    • Machine: an Apple M4 Pro (14 cores), on the CPU (JAX_PLATFORMS=cpu), on 2026-11-02.
    • Time: 75 minutes of wall time and 4.9 CPU-hours for 32 batches of 4 seeds. The registered estimate, 8-12 CPU-hours, was for all 60 batches; three comparisons were decided early.
    • Code: the first batch ran on main at ba5a070, which merged the pre-registration. Each later batch ran on the commit that recorded the batch before it. Those commits add lines to results.jsonl and change nothing else.
    • Deviations from the pre-registration: none. The settings, arms and test are the registered ones, and PREREG.md is unchanged. A first launch failed in the shell before training anything and recorded nothing.

    guidance.py report prints the tables above from results.jsonl, and guidance.py run reuses every recorded batch.
